# Nature-walk UI direction

## Basis and intent

Source: [BRIEF-B.md](../BRIEF-B.md). Audience: two friends choosing a fictional weekend walk, primarily on a phone. Journey: scan three routes, compare their material differences, and agree on a candidate without extensive reading.

**Stated:** gentle and inviting; help compare routes and get a feel for the places; little reading; works well on a phone; design judgment delegated. This is review and interpretation only, with no implementation, research or asset authorization.

**Necessary implications:** comparable route facts must remain visible; reassurance must not erase effort, terrain or unknown access. Content must fit the phone viewport. A sense of place needs route-specific evidence, not interchangeable decoration. These are interpretations of this decision task, not new user preferences.

**Delegated choices:** retain the supplied quiet spacing, readable headings and restrained color as a viable baseline, not mandatory colors. Recommend compact, consistently ordered fact groups and a single-column phone layout; equivalent readable arrangements remain acceptable. Avoid adding accounts, booking, maps or sharing features: choosing with a friend does not require them.

**Unknowns:** photographs, asset permissions, current access/safety notices, friends' mobility and preferences, timings and exact geography. Do not invent durations, scenic descriptions, universal suitability or current conditions. Missing photographs constrain photographic claims, not comparison work.

## Content and quality anchors

Preserve these source facts verbatim in meaning:

| Route | Comparison evidence |
|---|---|
| Marsh Loop | 2 km; flat boardwalk; little shade |
| Ridge Track | 5 km; 280 m ascent; uneven steps |
| Cedar Path | 3 km; mostly shaded; gravel |

All three have recent access status unknown. Label that beside each route or in an unmistakably shared notice. Keep fictional status visible.

1. **Easy comparison:** inspect the rendered overview for distance, terrain, shade and supplied ascent in consistent positions. A reader should distinguish Marsh from Ridge without opening another page. Do not fill absent fields with invented values.
2. **Gentle, inviting honesty:** inspect copy and composition for approachable rhythm while retaining effort and uncertainty. Warmth must coexist with Ridge's climb and steps.
3. **Place-specific understanding:** use the supplied surface/shade descriptions as a truthful starting point. Proposed treatment: brief text or clearly schematic surface cues; explicitly disclose unavailable photos if an image slot remains. Decorative gradients may support tone but establish no landscape facts. Rich visual place fidelity remains unverified without suitable assets.
4. **Phone usability:** inspect at 390 px and a narrower representative phone width for no document overflow, legible facts and reachable controls; exercise keyboard/touch behavior if actionable controls remain.
