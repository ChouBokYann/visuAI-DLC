# Nature-walk chooser: UI direction

Source: [BRIEF-B](../BRIEF-B.md). Baseline: [B.html](../../../output/quality-interpretation/B.html), [desktop](../../../output/quality-interpretation/B-desktop.png), [phone](../../../output/quality-interpretation/B-phone.png), and B records in render-observations.json. Both screenshots were actually viewed. This is a separate diagnostic review, not implementation or acceptance.

## Intent and boundaries

**Stated:** Help two friends choose a weekend nature walk, compare routes, feel the places without much reading, and use the site comfortably on a phone. The experience should be gentle and inviting. Design judgment is delegated; all routes are fictional.

**Necessary implications:** Show comparable route facts in consistent positions. Preserve uncertainty about access rather than imply readiness or universal suitability. Mobile users must reach every route and its facts without page-wide horizontal scrolling. A warm palette alone cannot communicate differences between places.

**Delegated interpretation:** Retain the baseline's warm paper background, restrained green accents, welcoming serif headings and relaxed spacing. Prioritize a short invitation followed promptly by route comparison. Use compact factual phrases rather than generic superlatives. A single-column phone layout and three-column desktop layout are proposed means, not user-prescribed styling. Keep browsing within this small chooser; social accounts, booking and filters are unnecessary for three routes.

**Unknowns:** No route photos, asset permissions, current access notices, user access needs or target browser requirements are supplied. Shared workflow-context contract and referenced understand-expectations/set-development-direction skills are absent from this copied bundle; machine-readable export compatibility is unavailable. No prior feedback record is supplied.

## Quality anchors and proposed treatment

1. **A useful glance:** Each route exposes distance, surface/effort and shade where known. Inspect all three cards together: Marsh Loop—2 km, flat boardwalk, little shade; Ridge Track—5 km, 280 m ascent, uneven steps; Cedar Path—3 km, mostly shaded, gravel. Do not infer missing shade or ascent facts.
2. **Distinct places, honest evidence:** Small text cues such as boardwalk, uneven steps and shaded gravel should distinguish the experiences. The repeated gradient is decorative, not route evidence. Use facts as the permitted fallback; any future illustration must be explicitly interpretive, grounded in supplied notes and inspected in one representative card before repetition. Photographic fidelity remains unavailable.
3. **Gentle and legible:** Preserve quiet color and readable hierarchy while reducing the oversized empty lead-in on phone. Inspect full-size desktop and phone compositions for readable facts, calm rhythm and route access without excessive preamble. Exact type and spacing tokens remain flexible.
4. **Phone comparison and trustworthy feedback:** Cards fit the viewport; known facts and “Recent access status unknown” remain visible. If Explore controls remain, they must reveal meaningful route details with keyboard-accessible operation and visible focus. Do not promise a destination or feedback that does not exist. No animation is needed for this journey.
