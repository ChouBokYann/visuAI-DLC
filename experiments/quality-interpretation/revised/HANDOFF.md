# Handoff for a different project: preserve intent through UI review

This is a self-contained reasoning handoff, not a design specification for your project. It comes from a synthetic review exercise involving a light, calm website for comparing afternoon workspaces. Later simulated feedback preferred actual working-area evidence, honest missing-photo states, and visible price/hours/power facts over generic imagery and omitted details. That feedback was not real user approval and establishes no preferences for your users.

## Transferable reasoning

Start with your project's actual user, decision and context. Separate explicit requirements, necessary implications, delegated design choices and unknowns. Translate a few consequential experience words into observations that would establish them. For example, “concise” means users can reach the needed decision without unnecessary reading; it does not automatically mean fewer words or fewer facts.

Identify the information and assets that make the experience convincing. Ask what each image, chart, diagram or interaction lets a user understand, and whether it actually supplies that evidence. Decoration can support tone, but should not masquerade as information about the subject. An honest unavailable state can be more trustworthy than a polished substitute. Missing evidence blocks the claim that depends on it, not all independent work.

Keep aesthetics, functional behavior and acceptance distinct. A screenshot can establish visible hierarchy or overflow; it cannot establish working navigation, trustworthy source data, motion quality or user acceptance. Inspect artifacts appropriate to each material claim and report Met, Not met or Unverified. An observed failure means Changes required; missing required evidence without an observed failure means Incomplete.

When feedback arrives, distinguish a newly revealed preference from an implementation defect or a previously weakened interpretation of the original goal. Preserve valid earlier evidence within its limits. Update the affected direction and checks, without silently changing unrelated requirements or treating feedback as permission to build.

## What does not transfer automatically

Cream/green styling, workspace photographs, daylight, seat views, opening hours, power access, day prices and a preference against photographic heroes were local to the exercise. Do not impose those attributes, a card layout, a fixed anchor count or a universal minimal-text aesthetic on another product. A photographic hero, dense text or diagram may be appropriate when your project's task calls for it.

## Apply to your next bounded increment

1. Read your own brief, existing conventions and available artifacts. Identify the representative journey, device/input context and the facts needed for the user's decision.
2. Record a few quality anchors with the exact artifact or interaction that would establish each. Label proposed treatments as agent judgment, keeping equivalent solutions flexible.
3. Check decisive assets and capabilities for relevance, suitability, permission and provenance. Choose a truthful fallback within your authority; leave only dependent claims unresolved.
4. Hand a specialist the intended experience, defining elements, preservation constraints, proposed treatments, missing inputs and concrete checks. Consult that specialist's available guidance; disclose missing guidance rather than claiming compatibility or installing anything automatically.
5. Inspect the result against the original goal as well as the brief. Record failures, evidence gaps and the next authorized action separately from human acceptance.

No input from the original workspace exercise is required to proceed. Use your project's own sources and authority. This handoff claims no specialist invocation, implementation, runtime verification or user acceptance; the trial's shared machine-readable contract was unavailable, so no export compatibility is implied.
