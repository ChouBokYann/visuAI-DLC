# Effect of pointing to prior feedback

All six implementers retrieved the relevant record and realized both preferences. The exact pointer reduced observed archive calls. Across the workspace, walk and rehearsal tasks the results were:

| Condition | Opened feedback | Calls through first feedback open | All archive calls | Original checks | Preference matches |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| History available | 3/3 | 6 | 15 | 12/12 | 6/6 |
| Exact pointer supplied | 3/3 | 3 | 4 | 12/12 | 6/6 |

Every unpointed worker listed the archive, opened the relevant review second, and subsequently opened all three remaining records. All pointed workers went straight to record 03. One also listed the archive afterward. Eleven fewer calls includes eight fewer calls *after* the relevant record was first opened. Call counts cannot establish latency or token savings.

A fresh scorer saw the source facts, item criteria, browser evidence and anonymous HTML. The scorer confirmed all six preference pairs, including shortest-first routes with access uncertainty before the choices, missing-photo and seat-daylight disclosure, and 12-hour rehearsal times with print styling. All six matched the original checked outcomes. Snapshot equality was checked before combining blinded scores with assignments.

The archive had four records, one plausibly titled Review meeting. Both arms were explicitly told the history interface existed. The pointer sentence also specified that the record contained prior user feedback, so the treatment bundles location and relevance. One sample per task and arm, instructional isolation, and synthetic records limit generalization. The UI exposed no measured failure that warrants adding mandatory workflow steps. The main unanswered question is selecting **current, applicable** feedback when several plausible records disagree.
