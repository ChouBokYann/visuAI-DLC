# Brief completeness pilot: frozen-rubric comparison

## Result

**The extra interpretation instruction did not demonstrate a consequential completeness benefit in this pilot.** Baseline already met all frozen initial criteria and incorporated later feedback without losing original goals. Candidate did the same. Both produced actionable text directions; neither establishes a working or aesthetically successful site.

The rubric was frozen before producer outputs were read. Initial evaluations used only original criteria; later-feedback requirements were evaluated only in fresh-agent revisions. Candidate skill/rationale and parent observations were not read by this evaluator. This is evaluation of the labelled conditions' outputs, not independent verification of their instruction delivery or mechanism.

Every later-feedback obligation was covered in both transfer conditions. Neither transfer lost or weakened an original user goal. No initial blocker was repaired because none was identified. These are separate stage scores, not a combined improvement index.

## Concrete differences without hindsight

Candidate initial coffee makes drinks versus retail beans explicit and warns that a generic cafe homepage may not support an origin association. Baseline already requires matched-origin source evidence and historical/current distinctions. Candidate also names a route away from sparse origins; baseline provides an honest empty state and alternative origin navigation. These are useful elaborations, not repairs to a demonstrated baseline failure.

Candidate initial ceramics explicitly labels full/cancelled/past sessions and illustrates why a generic weekly schedule is insufficient. Baseline already specifies actual dates/start/end times and source-dated availability. Both identify later collection/visits and total commitment. Counterexample labels alone do not earn points.

Specificity is not uniformly higher in candidate: baseline initial ceramics explicitly requests availability source/checked date, while candidate leaves those details less developed until transfer. Both still satisfy initial planning requirements. Both transfers provide clear recommendation eligibility, uncertainty handling, cost/beginner information and the coffee scene/focus/comparison behavior.

Candidate transfer coffee calls the inherited no-checkout choice a constraint, although the coffee user did not explicitly require it. It also labels mechanics proposed rather than user-approved. This minor provenance ambiguity neither establishes fabricated acceptance nor blocks the requested journey; it is not a hard failure or grounds for a correction under the frozen rule.

## Limits and decision supported

- Two cases and one observed output per case/condition/stage are too few to establish general superiority, equivalence or absence of benefit elsewhere. No repeated independent trials or uncertainty estimates were produced.
- Baseline reaches the scoring ceiling. A tie cannot show that the instruction never helps; it shows no detected benefit against these frozen actionable-completeness criteria.
- The evaluator knew which outputs were baseline/candidate and read baseline first. Frozen criteria constrain hindsight but do not eliminate order or expectation effects. No independent second rater was used.
- Synthetic feedback tests explicit follow-through. Its successful incorporation does not show that a producer could anticipate every unstated user preference. Later requirements were never imposed retroactively.
- Fresh-agent self-contained handoffs were judged as text. No actual implementer attempted the build, and proposed checks were not executed. Data freshness, source validity, licensed imagery, rendered usability and visual aesthetics remain unverified.
- Tool/skill execution and instruction compliance were not independently audited through restricted output reads. No causal mechanism for any prose difference can be established.
- No objective aesthetic score is claimed. Multiple visual treatments can satisfy the same journey and constraints.

**Supported decision:** Do not describe the extra interpretation instruction as a proven fix or require it on evidence from this pilot. Retain it only as an optional hypothesis if its extra process cost is acceptable; a broader, prospectively defined evaluation would be needed to justify a general requirement. This pilot supplies no substantive correction request and no observed baseline omission demanding a refinement.
